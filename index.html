SpokenSlate

Transcripts tell you what the professor said. SpokenSlate tells you what they meant.

A recorded whiteboard lecture, turned into a page a screen reader can follow: every board step in order, and spoken references like “this term cancels” linked to the exact thing on the board. When it isn’t sure, it says unresolved instead of guessing.

Reading a page with a screen reader

  • Each board step is a heading. Jump between steps with your screen reader’s heading key (VoiceOver: Control-Option-Command-H; NVDA and JAWS: H).
  • Each step starts with a plain-language reading, then the math itself, which you can explore cell by cell, then the LaTeX source behind a “LaTeX” toggle.
  • In the speech, a linked reference says what it means, for example “this (means minus 2, in step 3)”. Follow the link to jump to that step.
  • “Unresolved” means the system wasn’t confident. It may add “possibly step N”, which is a hint, not a claim.
  • Steps that were erased on the board are kept and labelled.

How it works

Meta’s SAM 3.1 masks the lecturer out of each board image; Muse Spark reads the board into steps and decides what each spoken reference points at, using the words at that instant, the lecturer’s fingertip, and frames before and after. Every answer passes a validator; anything uncertain becomes “unresolved”.
